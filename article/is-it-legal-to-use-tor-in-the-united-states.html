Is It Legal to Use Tor in the United States?

Using the Tor network and the Tor Browser is completely legal in the United States. Simply downloading the software, browsing the web through encrypted relays, and visiting both standard websites and hidden services (.onion sites) does not violate any federal or state laws. However, while the anonymity tool itself is legal, using Tor to engage in unlawful activities remains strictly illegal and subject to criminal prosecution.

Tor Is a Legal Privacy Tool

In the United States, there are no laws prohibiting the use of encryption tools, proxy networks, or the Tor Browser. The U.S. government, particularly the U.S. Naval Research Laboratory and the State Department, originally funded and helped develop the Tor project to protect sensitive government communications and support free expression worldwide.

Under U.S. law, citizens and residents have a legal right to digital privacy and encrypted communications. Millions of individuals use Tor legally every day, including:

  • Journalists and Whistleblowers: To communicate securely with sources without fear of surveillance.
  • Law Enforcement and Military Personnel: To conduct investigations, gather intelligence, and operate undercover without revealing their physical locations.
  • Everyday Citizens: To prevent internet service providers (ISPs), advertisers, and websites from tracking their browsing habits.
  • Cybersecurity Professionals: To perform security assessments, vulnerability testing, and threat analysis.

The Distinction Between the Tool and the Action

The legality of Tor depends entirely on how it is used. Tor functions like any other internet browser, vehicle, or communication tool: the technology is neutral, but the user's actions determine legality.

  • Legal Actions on Tor: Reading news, browsing privacy forums, accessing clearnet sites anonymously, and viewing non-illicit hidden services.
  • Illegal Actions on Tor: Purchasing or selling illicit substances, distributing copyrighted or illegal material, coordinating cyberattacks, financial fraud, and unauthorized access to computer systems.

Law enforcement agencies such as the FBI, DEA, and Homeland Security actively monitor illegal darknet marketplaces. If an individual commits a crime using Tor, they can be prosecuted for that crime under standard federal and state statutes.

The Legality of Running Tor Nodes

Beyond using the browser, running a Tor node inside the United States is also legal, though the specific type of node carries different implications:

  • Middle and Guard Relays: Completely legal and safe to host. These servers only pass encrypted traffic between other Tor nodes and do not interact directly with destination websites.
  • Exit Nodes: Legal to operate, but operators face significant practical risks. Because an exit node is the final relay that forwards unencrypted traffic to destination websites, the operator’s IP address will be associated with any illegal activity passing through that node. While exit node operators are generally shielded from liability under the Digital Millennium Copyright Act (DMCA) and common carrier principles, they may still receive copyright notices or law enforcement inquiries.

Summary

Using Tor in the United States is fully legal. The software is a legitimate encryption platform designed for privacy and free speech. As long as users do not engage in criminal behavior, they can download, run, and browse with Tor without breaking the law.
