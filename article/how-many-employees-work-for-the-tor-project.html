How Many Employees Work for the Tor Project?

The Tor Project operates as a 501(c)(3) non-profit organization with a surprisingly lean core team. Despite maintaining one of the world's most critical internet privacy networks, the organization typically employs between 35 and 50 full-time equivalent staff members and dedicated contractors. This core team is augmented by a vast global community of thousands of independent volunteers who run relays, translate software, and contribute open-source code.

Core Workforce Breakdown

The Tor Project maintains a distributed workforce spanning multiple continents. The core paid personnel consists of:

  • Full-Time Staff and Contractors: Between 35 and 50 individuals are actively compensated for full-time or dedicated part-time roles. This includes software developers, network engineers, system administrators, user experience (UX) designers, community coordinators, and administrative/fundraising staff.
  • Geographic Distribution: Most staff members work remotely across North America, South America, and Europe, allowing the non-profit to operate without a single massive central office.

Operational Roles within the Organization

The paid team is divided into several specialized functional areas:

  1. Browser and Core Software Development: Engineers responsible for maintaining Tor Browser, C-Tor, and the newer Rust-based Arti implementation.
  2. Network Health and Metrics: Specialists focused on directory authorities, bandwidth scanning, bridge distribution, and anti-censorship tools like pluggable transports.
  3. Community and Localization: Coordinators managing user support, documentation, bug triage, and community outreach in heavily censored regions.
  4. Operations and Governance: Leadership, financial officers, legal counsel, and grant managers who ensure compliance with non-profit regulations and manage grant-funded milestones.

The Role of the Volunteer Network

The Tor Project relies heavily on a hybrid model where paid staff oversee core infrastructure while volunteers handle decentralized scaling:

  • Relay Operators: The Tor network relies on over 7,000 volunteer-run relays and thousands of bridges worldwide. These operators are not Tor Project employees; they independently donate bandwidth, servers, and electricity.
  • Open-Source Contributors: Independent developers submit patches, audit security, report vulnerabilities, and assist with language localizations.

By maintaining a core group of roughly 35 to 50 paid specialists, the Tor Project is able to direct the majority of its grant and donation funding toward vital software engineering and anti-censorship research while leveraging a global community to sustain network capacity.
