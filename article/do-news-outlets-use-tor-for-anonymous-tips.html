Do News Outlets Use Tor for Anonymous Tips?

Major news organizations around the world actively promote the use of the Tor network to safeguard whistleblowers, confidential sources, and journalists. By deploying Tor-based submission platforms like SecureDrop and hosting dedicated hidden services (.onion sites), prominent publishers ensure that sensitive documents and tips can be transmitted without capturing identifying metadata, IP addresses, or location data. This infrastructure allows sources to share public-interest information safely while minimizing the risk of surveillance and retaliation.

Leading investigative outlets—including The New York Times, The Washington Post, The Guardian, ProPublica, and The Intercept—maintain dedicated SecureDrop servers accessible exclusively via the Tor network. SecureDrop, an open-source system originally developed by Aaron Swartz and managed by the Freedom of the Press Foundation, is designed to eliminate the digital paper trail associated with traditional communications. Media organizations prominently feature links and instructions for these platforms on their official "Tips" or "Contact Us" pages.

News organizations favor Tor because standard digital communication methods, such as unencrypted emails, standard web forms, and cellular networks, inherently generate logs and metadata. Even when the content of a message is encrypted, standard protocols reveal who contacted whom, when, and from what location. Tor solves this problem by routing encrypted traffic through three distinct, volunteer-operated nodes, effectively isolating the sender's identity from the recipient and any eavesdroppers along the route.

In addition to providing Tor-accessible platforms, newsrooms routinely publish comprehensive security guides to help sources protect themselves. These instructions advise users to download the official Tor Browser, connect through public Wi-Fi networks rather than home or corporate networks, use privacy-focused operating systems such as Tails, and avoid sharing identifiable details about their submission on regular communication channels.

Using the Tor network has become a standard protocol for investigative reporting. By actively promoting Tor-based platforms, media organizations provide a secure conduit for high-risk leaks, protecting the anonymity of sources while upholding modern press freedom standards.
