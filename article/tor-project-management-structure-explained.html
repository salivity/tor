Tor Project Management Structure Explained

The Tor Project is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit organization governed by a multi-tiered management model comprising a Board of Directors, executive leadership, functional core teams, and a decentralized community of open-source contributors. This structure balances corporate governance and legal accountability with the transparency and collaboration required of an open-source privacy network.

Board of Directors

At the highest level of governance is the Board of Directors. The Board holds fiduciary and legal responsibility for the Tor Project. It does not manage day-to-day software development or operations, but instead focuses on long-term strategy, financial health, policy compliance, organizational health, and appointing executive leadership. The Board consists of independent experts in privacy, digital rights, computer security, and organizational governance.

Executive Leadership

Day-to-day operations are led by the Executive Director, who acts as the primary liaison between the Board of Directors and the operational staff. The Executive Director is responsible for executing the organization's strategic vision, securing funding, managing global operations, and representing the Tor Project publicly. The Executive Director is supported by leadership roles across core operational areas, including engineering, communications, and finance.

Core Teams and Paid Staff

Beneath executive leadership, the Tor Project employs a distributed team of staff and core contractors organized into functional teams:

  • Applications Team: Focuses on user-facing software, primarily the Tor Browser for desktop and mobile platforms.
  • Network & Core Engineering: Maintains the core Tor protocol, routing architecture, cryptography, and network performance.
  • Anti-Censorship Team: Develops pluggable transports and bypass technologies to ensure access in heavily censored regions.
  • Network Health: Monitors the health, consensus, and security of the volunteer-run relay network.
  • Community and User Support: Provides direct assistance to users, coordinates outreach, localized documentation, and trainer training worldwide.
  • Operations and Fundraising: Manages accounting, grant reporting, donor relations, and human resources.

Community and Relay Operators

While the nonprofit coordinates development and maintenance, the underlying Tor network is decentralized. Independent volunteers, universities, and organizations operate the thousands of guard, middle, and exit relays that route traffic. Technical changes and protocol upgrades follow an open Tor Proposal process, allowing developers, security researchers, and community members to propose, debate, and review network modifications publicly.
