When Did the EFF Begin Supporting Tor?

The Electronic Frontier Foundation (EFF) officially began supporting the Tor Project in late 2004. This collaboration provided critical financial, legal, and organizational backing during Tor's transition from a government-funded research experiment into a widely accessible open-source privacy network, helping establish it as a cornerstone tool for online anonymity and free expression.

The Origins and the 2004 Transition

Tor, short for The Onion Router, was initially designed and developed in the mid-1990s by mathematician Paul Syverson and computer scientists Michael G. Reed and David Goldschlag at the U.S. Naval Research Laboratory (NRL), with additional funding from DARPA. The primary goal was to create a secure routing system to protect government communications.

By 2004, the Naval Research Laboratory released the underlying code for Tor under a free, open-source software license. To function effectively as an anonymity network, Tor needed a diverse user base composed of ordinary citizens, activists, and businesses rather than just government personnel, because a network used exclusively by intelligence agencies would defeat its own purpose of hiding individual user identities.

EFF's Role and Sponsorship

Recognizing the vital role that anonymity tools play in defending digital civil liberties, the Electronic Frontier Foundation stepped in during the latter half of 2004. The EFF became Tor’s primary fiscal sponsor, providing the project with direct funding, technical resources, and web hosting.

The EFF's involvement achieved several key objectives:

  • Financial Stability: EFF grants allowed core developers Roger Dingledine and Nick Mathewson to work on the protocol full-time.
  • Public Advocacy: The EFF promoted onion routing to journalists, human rights defenders, and privacy advocates worldwide.
  • Legal Defense: The EFF offered legal guidance to early node operators and helped establish protections for running privacy infrastructure.

The Path to Independence

EFF maintained direct sponsorship through 2005. With the foundational support provided by the EFF, the developers formally incorporated The Tor Project as an independent 501(c)(3) nonprofit organization in December 2006.

Although the Tor Project now operates as an autonomous organization with diverse funding sources, the EFF remains one of its most vocal supporters and continues to advocate for the use of privacy-enhancing technologies globally.
