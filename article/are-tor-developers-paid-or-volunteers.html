Are Tor Developers Paid or Volunteers?

The development of the Tor network relies on a hybrid model that includes both paid professionals and unpaid volunteers. The core organization behind the software, The Tor Project, employs a dedicated team of paid software engineers, researchers, and staff. At the same time, the wider ecosystem depends heavily on an active global community of open-source volunteer developers, translators, and relay operators.

Paid Developers at The Tor Project

The Tor Project is registered as a 501(c)(3) nonprofit organization based in the United States. It employs full-time and contract staff who receive regular salaries to maintain and improve the privacy software.

Paid developers are responsible for critical tasks, including:

  • Core development of the Tor network protocol and Tor Browser.
  • Security patches, continuous code audits, and vulnerability mitigation.
  • Network architecture, cryptography research, and anti-censorship tools like pluggable transports.

Funding for these salaries comes primarily from government research grants, private foundations, corporate sponsorships, and individual donations.

The Role of Volunteer Contributors

While the core staff manages primary releases and critical infrastructure, volunteers play an essential role in expanding and supporting the ecosystem.

Volunteer contributions include:

  • Code Contributions: Independent developers submit patches, new features, and bug fixes to Tor’s open-source repositories.
  • Localization: Translators adapt the Tor Browser and documentation into dozens of languages.
  • Testing and Bug Reporting: Community members test alpha and nightly builds to identify issues before public releases.
  • Related Tools: Many auxiliary tools, libraries, and third-party applications interacting with Tor are built entirely by independent, unpaid developers.

Relay Operators

It is also important to distinguish software developers from node operators. The physical servers that route Tor traffic—known as relays and exit nodes—are operated almost entirely by unpaid volunteers and independent organizations worldwide. These operators donate their own hardware, bandwidth, and time to keep the network decentralized and operational.

In summary, core Tor development is led by a paid team of professionals employed by The Tor Project, while the broader development, localization, and network infrastructure are sustained by global volunteers.
