Corporate Sponsors That Back the Tor Project

The Tor Project relies on a diverse funding model that includes financial grants, infrastructural support, and donations from several prominent corporations. While historically known for receiving substantial public and non-profit grants, private sector companies—particularly in the technology, search, and cybersecurity industries—regularly provide crucial financial backing and in-kind services to support the maintenance, development, and expansion of Tor's privacy network.

Major Corporate Sponsors

Over the years, several well-known technology companies have contributed directly to the Tor Project through grants, bug bounty rewards, and recurring donations:

  • Google: Provided financial grants through various open-source initiatives and historically supported developer participation through programs like the Google Summer of Code.
  • DuckDuckGo: A long-time privacy partner and recurring financial donor, regularly contributing privacy-focused grants to support Tor's core software and relay network.
  • Mozilla: Awarded substantial funding through the Mozilla Open Source Support (MOSS) program to enhance the Tor network’s performance, security, and scalability.
  • Fastly and Cloudflare: Provide technical infrastructure, content delivery network (CDN) services, and network routing support to help Tor manage bandwidth and resist distributed denial-of-service (DDoS) attacks.
  • Team Cymru and Disconnect: Have provided enterprise-level threat intelligence, data feeds, and direct sponsorship to assist Tor in identifying malicious relays.

Types of Corporate Support

Corporate backing for Tor generally falls into three distinct categories:

  1. Direct Financial Grants: Unrestricted or project-specific capital used to pay core software developers, security auditors, and community outreach teams.
  2. Infrastructure and In-Kind Contributions: High-speed server capacity, transit bandwidth, domain name services, and DDoS mitigation tools that lower the operational costs of maintaining network directories and website mirrors.
  3. Open-Source Collaborations: Joint research initiatives, vulnerability disclosure programs, and engineering hours dedicated to improving upstream open-source code shared between Tor and privacy-focused browsers like Firefox and Brave.

Financial Transparency

The Tor Project operates as a 501(c)(3) research and education non-profit organization based in the United States. To maintain operational transparency and user trust, the organization publicly discloses its financial statements, Form 990 tax filings, and an annual list of all corporate, governmental, and individual donors contributing over designated financial thresholds.
