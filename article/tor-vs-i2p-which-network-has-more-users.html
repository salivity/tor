Tor vs I2P: Which Network Has More Users?

When comparing anonymous overlay networks, the Tor network has a vastly larger user base than I2P (the Invisible Internet Project). While both platforms are designed to protect user privacy and resist censorship, Tor consistently records millions of daily active users, whereas I2P maintains a much smaller, highly specialized community of tens of thousands of active nodes. This article examines the user metrics of both networks and explains the key factors driving this substantial difference in adoption.

Tor User Base and Metrics

The Tor network is currently the most widely used anonymity network in the world. According to public metrics provided by the Tor Project, the network routinely supports between 2.5 million and 4 million daily connected users across the globe.

Tor achieves these numbers through a large volunteer infrastructure consisting of over 7,000 relay nodes and roughly 2,000 bridge relays designed to bypass network censorship. Its widespread adoption spans privacy advocates, journalists, researchers, and individuals seeking to bypass geographical content restrictions or government firewalls.

I2P User Base and Metrics

The Invisible Internet Project (I2P) operates on a significantly smaller scale. At any given time, the active number of I2P routers (nodes) typically ranges between 30,000 and 50,000.

Because every client participating in the I2P network also functions as a router for other traffic, the total number of active routers closely mirrors the total active user count. While accurate long-term statistics are harder to pinpoint due to the decentralized and private nature of the protocol, I2P's user numbers remain roughly two orders of magnitude smaller than Tor's.

Why Tor Has a Larger User Base

Several key architectural and usability factors contribute to Tor’s dominance in user adoption:

  • Turnkey Usability: Tor offers the Tor Browser, a pre-configured, standalone application based on Mozilla Firefox. Users can download, install, and begin browsing the web anonymously within minutes without configuring proxy settings.
  • Access to the Clearnet: Tor is primarily engineered to provide secure, anonymous access to the regular internet (clearnet) via exit relays, alongside its internal "Onion Services." Most general users use Tor to browse standard websites privately.
  • Internal Network Focus of I2P: I2P was built as a closed, self-contained network ("darknet") intended for internal communication, peer-to-peer file sharing, and hosting decentralized services ("eepsites"). Outbound access to the regular internet requires limited, third-party outproxies, making it less appealing to typical web users.
  • Configuration Complexity: Setting up I2P historically required configuring separate local browser proxies, software daemons, or specialized application tunnels, which creates a barrier to entry for non-technical users.
  • Funding and Public Recognition: The Tor Project operates as an established non-profit organization with dedicated staff, grants, and mainstream media recognition, giving it significantly higher visibility than I2P's developer-driven volunteer model.

Summary

Tor is substantially larger than I2P in terms of user volume, supporting millions of daily users compared to I2P’s tens of thousands. While I2P remains an effective and resilient solution for decentralized, internal peer-to-peer networking, Tor remains the standard choice for general-purpose anonymous internet browsing.
