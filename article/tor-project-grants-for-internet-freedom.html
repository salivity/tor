Tor Project Grants for Internet Freedom

The Tor Project operates as a 501(c)(3) nonprofit organization that relies heavily on institutional grants to maintain its privacy network, build anti-censorship software, and promote digital human rights globally. These grants, provided by government agencies, non-governmental organizations, and private foundations, directly fund the development of open-source tools that protect journalists, activists, and citizens living under repressive regimes.

Open Technology Fund (OTF)

The Open Technology Fund has historically been one of Tor's primary financial backers for circumvention and privacy research. OTF grants have supported multiple core internet freedom initiatives, including:

  • Pluggable Transports and Circumvention: Funding the development and deployment of protocols like obfs4, meek, and Snowflake, which disguise Tor traffic as standard web traffic to bypass sophisticated national firewalls.
  • Network Infrastructure and Performance: Enhancing the capacity, speed, and reliability of the distributed relay network to support users in bandwidth-constrained regions.
  • Security Audits and Code Refactoring: Sustaining ongoing maintenance to ensure the software remains secure against state-sponsored exploits.

U.S. Department of State (DRL)

The Bureau of Democracy, Human Rights, and Labor (DRL) within the U.S. Department of State has awarded Tor several multi-year grants focused on anti-censorship and digital safety:

  • Bypass and Resilience Tooling: Upgrading bridge distribution mechanisms, such as Telegram- and email-based bridge distribution, to help users access the network when the primary Tor website is blocked.
  • User Training and Localization: Translating the Tor Browser and documentation into languages such as Persian, Russian, Burmese, and Arabic, alongside funding direct digital security training for human rights defenders.

Swedish International Development Cooperation Agency (Sida)

Sida has provided substantial grant funding to expand Tor's reach across the Global South and areas facing severe internet restrictions. Key initiatives funded include:

  • Community Outreach and User Feedback: Establishing community teams to collect direct feedback from human rights organizations operating in high-risk jurisdictions.
  • Mobile Ecosystem Support: Advancing the development of Tor Browser for Android, ensuring users who rely entirely on mobile devices can access the free web securely.

European and Foreign Government Grants

Various European state departments and innovation funds have awarded grants for privacy-preserving software infrastructure:

  • German Federal Foreign Office: Supported localized circumvention tooling and privacy integration projects.
  • Prototype Fund (Germany): Provided targeted grants for independent developers to build tools enhancing Tor ecosystem usability and network diagnostics.
  • European Commission (Horizon 2020): Funded collaborative research into distributed systems, anonymity metrics, and cryptographic improvements.

Mozilla Open Source Support (MOSS)

The Mozilla Foundation has granted Tor funding through its MOSS program to modernize its technical stack:

  • The Arti Project: Funding the migration of Tor's core codebase from C to Rust to enhance memory safety, modularity, and integration into other applications.
  • Network Performance Enhancements: Grants aimed at optimizing the Tor Browser’s performance, reducing latency, and improving onion service accessibility.

Private Foundations and Philanthropic Funds

Tor frequently receives non-governmental grants from technology and civil liberty foundations:

  • NLnet Foundation: Financed specific open-source enhancements, onion routing upgrades, and next-generation decentralized protocol designs.
  • Ford Foundation and Knight Foundation: Awarded grants aimed at strengthening open-source governance, digital rights advocacy, and the protection of investigative journalism platforms built on top of Tor (such as SecureDrop).
