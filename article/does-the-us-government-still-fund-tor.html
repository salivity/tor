Does the US Government Still Fund Tor?

The United States government continues to provide substantial financial support to the Tor Project through federal grants, research initiatives, and federally funded non-profit organizations. While Tor has actively diversified its revenue stream in recent years with individual donations and private foundations, public financial disclosures confirm that U.S. government-backed funding remains a major contributor to the organization's annual budget to promote global internet freedom and combat censorship.

The Origins of Tor and Federal Funding

The Tor (The Onion Router) technology was originally developed in the mid-1990s by the U.S. Naval Research Laboratory and the Defense Advanced Research Projects Agency (DARPA) to protect sensitive government communications. In 2004, the Naval Research Laboratory released the source code under a free license, leading to the creation of The Tor Project as a 501(c)(3) research and educational non-profit in 2006. Since its inception as an independent entity, federal agencies have maintained an active financial interest in maintaining the network.

Current Government Funding Channels

Today, federal funding for Tor is routed primarily through agencies dedicated to international development, scientific research, and global civil liberties. Key sources include:

  • The Open Technology Fund (OTF): Originally funded through Radio Free Asia under the U.S. Agency for Global Media (USAGM), the OTF is an independent non-profit that receives federal appropriations to support internet freedom projects worldwide. It remains one of Tor’s most consistent grant providers.
  • The U.S. Department of State: Through the Bureau of Democracy, Human Rights, and Labor (DRL), the State Department awards grants to Tor to improve tools that bypass digital censorship in authoritarian countries.
  • The National Science Foundation (NSF): The NSF awards competitive research grants to the Tor Project and partnered academic institutions to study cryptographic systems, network routing, and internet security.
  • Defense Advanced Research Projects Agency (DARPA): DARPA periodically funds specific technical research projects related to privacy-preserving network architectures.

Financial Breakdown and Budget Reliance

According to the Tor Project’s publicly available Form 990 tax filings and annual financial reports:

  • Historically, U.S. government funding accounted for roughly 80% to 90% of Tor's total revenue in the late 2000s and early 2010s.
  • In recent years, that figure has dropped to between 30% and 50% of total revenue.
  • The remainder of Tor's funding comes from individual donors, corporate sponsors, and private philanthropic foundations.

Why the U.S. Government Funds Tor

The federal government supports Tor primarily because the network serves strategic foreign policy and national security goals:

  1. Circumventing Censorship: Tor allows citizens, journalists, and political dissidents in restrictive regimes to access unfiltered information and communicate securely.
  2. Operational Anonymity: For U.S. government personnel, investigators, and intelligence operatives to browse the web anonymously, they must hide within a large crowd of civilian users. A private, military-only network would make any traffic coming from it immediately identifiable.
  3. Open-Source Infrastructure: Supporting public open-source software aligns with broader federal initiatives to enhance global cybersecurity standards.

Does Government Funding Compromise Tor?

Despite heavy government funding, Tor remains an open-source project. Its source code is completely public, subject to independent audits by external cryptographers and security researchers. The decentralized structure of the network—operated by thousands of volunteer-run relays around the world—ensures that funding sources do not translate to architectural backdoors or direct control over the network's data traffic.
